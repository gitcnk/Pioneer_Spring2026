### Final Paper Guidelines:


### Do's 

- Strive to come up with an analysis that has a `flow' rather than a set of disjointed analyses.  This may need a lot of planning, redoing and rewriting.

-  Provide a reasonable rationale for each of the research questions that you investigate.

- Provide adequate numerical and graphical justifications for your claims and interpret your findings in context.

-  Discuss any issues that you see in your analysis and suggest some ways to improve or strengthen your analysis.  Note: A familiar `claim' by students (and researchers) is to ``get a large sample".  I don't think this is helpful to anyone, because any reasonable person knows that ALL  findings are subjected to the limitations in sample size.

-  Be brave and try to use methods that we have not used in class.  I will not penalize you for getting these wrong, the objective is to learn how to use them on your own.

---------



### Dont's 


-  Don't dump your R-outputs into your report.  Use only the relevant parts in summary form.

-  Don't perform  statistical tests without any purpose.  Remember, if you do LOTS of `tests' you will find ``something''.

-  Don't repeat what is obvious.  For example, if you see a positive correlation in your scatterplot, don't reiterate it in words.  Explain why (or why not) it makes sense and think about what underlying factors may have lead to such a correlation.



--------



### Grading Policy

You will be graded based on my qualitative assessment of your paper.  The rubric is below.


A: Demonstrates superior understanding of the concepts.  Outstanding written communication skills. Ideas are highly original and/or wildly creative.  Explanations are impeccably organized.

B: Demonstrates solid understanding of the concepts.  Good written communication skills. Ideas are original and creative. Explanations are well-organized.

C: Demonstrates basic understanding of the concepts.  Decent written communication skills. Ideas are interesting but predictable. Explanations are fairly well-organized.

D: Demonstrates lack of basic understanding of the concepts.  Weak written communication skills, and/or poorly organized explanations.

F: Demonstrates little.
